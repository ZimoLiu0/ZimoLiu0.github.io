2D Materials

Intercalation Engineering of Ferroic Properties in 2D Materials

Using molecular and atomic intercalation to control magnetic ordering and switchable polarization in layered materials.

Scientific motivation

The interlayer space in transition-metal dichalcogenides offers a route to modify symmetry and interfacial coupling. This project examines whether molecular and atomic guests can induce ferroic behavior in layered hosts.

My contributions

Grew TaS₂ single crystals and synthesized cobaltocene-intercalated TaS₂. Used structural and magnetic measurements to check intercalation and characterize the resulting materials.

Proposed site-selective Cu intercalation in TaS₂, synthesized Cu-intercalated samples, performed piezoresponse force microscopy, and used VASP to investigate stable interlayer configurations.

Methods

  • Chemical vapor transport and solvothermal intercalation
  • X-ray diffraction, Raman spectroscopy, vibrating-sample magnetometry, and magnetic force microscopy
  • Piezoresponse force microscopy and first-principles structural optimization in VASP

Selected results & status

The published molecular-intercalation study reports room-temperature ferromagnetism in a family of layered materials.

In the ongoing Cu-intercalation study, I observed ferroelectric switching and domain writing by piezoresponse force microscopy. Calculations identified two energetically stable interlayer sites. I am exploring how the approach extends to other transition-metal dichalcogenides.
